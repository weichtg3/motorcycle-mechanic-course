## Read

This lesson focuses on **v-twin layouts: sportster, big twin and honda**. The goal is not memorizing isolated parts; it is learning to see a motorcycle as a chain of inputs, mechanisms and outputs. Shared V-twin principles with different architecture, packaging and service access. On older motorcycles, that systems view matters because a symptom at one component is often caused somewhere else.

A four-stroke engine completes intake, compression, power and exhaust events over two crankshaft revolutions. The valves must open and close at the correct times, the piston/rings must seal enough pressure, the mixture must be combustible, and ignition must occur at the right moment. If any of these are missing, the engine may crank normally yet fail to run.

Compression and leak-down tests are not magic verdicts; they are comparative tools. A low reading can be affected by cranking speed, throttle position, gauge technique and engine temperature. Use the factory procedure and compare cylinders where appropriate. Mechanical noise also needs context—cold versus hot, engine speed versus road speed, load versus no load.

## Compare the four bikes

### 🏍 1995 Sportster 883 Hugger
The **1995 Harley-Davidson Sportster 883 Hugger** is our compact Harley reference: an air-cooled, carbureted Evolution 883 V-twin with a five-speed gearbox and belt final drive. Its relatively exposed layout makes it especially useful for learning mechanical relationships. Exact specifications and procedures must be confirmed against the correct 1995 factory literature for the VIN/configuration.

### 🏍 1996 Ultra Classic
The **1996 Harley-Davidson Electra Glide Ultra Classic (FLHTCU)** adds touring complexity. It uses a carbureted 1340 Evolution engine and belt final drive, but the fairing, luggage, touring electrical loads and greater mass make access, electrical diagnosis and chassis work more demanding. A current parts reference lists the 1340 carbureted Evolution engine and belt drive for this model, but factory literature remains authoritative.

### 🏍 1998 Shadow Aero 1100
The **1998 Honda Shadow Aero 1100** gives us a deliberately different engineering approach: Honda documented an 1,099 cc liquid-cooled four-stroke V-twin, five-speed transmission and shaft final drive. That makes it ideal for comparing cooling, packaging and final-drive service with the Harleys.

### 🏍 2025 Road Glide
The **2025 Harley-Davidson Road Glide** is our modern benchmark. Harley-Davidson specifies a 117 cu in Milwaukee-Eight 117 V-twin, Electronic Sequential Port Fuel Injection (ESPFI), liquid-cooled cylinder heads, six-speed gearing, belt final drive, selectable ride modes, rider-safety electronics and a 12.3-inch TFT running Skyline OS. Its Milwaukee-Eight 117 remains a four-stroke V-twin, but higher output, electronic fuel control and liquid-cooled cylinder heads show how the same combustion fundamentals are managed in a modern touring engine. Exact service values and procedures must come from the correct 2025 factory literature.
