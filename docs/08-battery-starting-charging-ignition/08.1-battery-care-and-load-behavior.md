## Read

This lesson focuses on **battery care and load behavior**. The goal is not memorizing isolated parts; it is learning to see a motorcycle as a chain of inputs, mechanisms and outputs. Battery chemistry basics, terminal care, parasitic drain and load behavior. On older motorcycles, that systems view matters because a symptom at one component is often caused somewhere else.

Electrical diagnosis becomes much easier when you stop treating electricity as invisible magic. A circuit needs a source, a complete path through the load, and a return path. A meter should answer a specific question: is voltage present, is the path capable of carrying current, or is resistance where it should not be? Continuity testing with power removed is useful, but a loaded voltage-drop test often finds corrosion and poor connections that a continuity beep misses.

Work from the battery outward. Confirm the battery is charged and connections are physically sound before condemning a starter, coil, stator or regulator. Older motorcycles are especially sensitive to grounds, connector corrosion and previous accessory wiring. Never move meter leads between voltage and current jacks casually; an ammeter connected across a battery can create a dangerous short.

### Garage exercise

Measure resting voltage, then observe voltage during starting if the bike is safe to crank.

Document what you find. If you perform actual service, record date, mileage, parts/fluids used, measurements before/after, and any follow-up required.

## Compare the four bikes

### 🏍 1997 Sportster 883 Hugger
The **1997 Harley-Davidson Sportster 883 Hugger** is our compact Harley reference: an air-cooled, carbureted Evolution 883 V-twin with a five-speed gearbox and belt final drive. Its relatively exposed layout makes it especially useful for learning mechanical relationships. Exact specifications and procedures must be confirmed against the correct 1997 factory literature for the VIN/configuration.

### 🏍 1995 Ultra Classic
The **1995 Harley-Davidson Electra Glide Ultra Classic (FLHTCU)** adds touring complexity. It uses a carbureted 1340 Evolution engine and belt final drive, but the fairing, luggage, touring electrical loads and greater mass make access, electrical diagnosis and chassis work more demanding. A current parts reference lists the 1340 carbureted Evolution engine and belt drive for this model, but factory literature remains authoritative.

### 🏍 1998 Shadow Aero 1100
The **1998 Honda Shadow Aero 1100** gives us a deliberately different engineering approach: Honda documented an 1,099 cc liquid-cooled four-stroke V-twin, five-speed transmission and shaft final drive. That makes it ideal for comparing cooling, packaging and final-drive service with the Harleys.

### 🏍 2025 Road Glide
The **2025 Harley-Davidson Road Glide** is our modern benchmark. Harley-Davidson specifies a 117 cu in Milwaukee-Eight 117 V-twin, Electronic Sequential Port Fuel Injection (ESPFI), liquid-cooled cylinder heads, six-speed gearing, belt final drive, selectable ride modes, rider-safety electronics and a 12.3-inch TFT running Skyline OS. Modern electronics do not replace basic battery/starting/charging tests. They make stable system voltage more important because low voltage can create misleading module and communication symptoms. Exact service values and procedures must come from the correct 2025 factory literature.
